## 4. Optimization Recommendations

Even though the network is healthy, proactive tuning can further improve resilience and prepare for future growth.

| Area | Recommendation | Rationale |
|------|----------------|-----------|
| **Monitoring Granularity** | Increase sampling frequency (e.g., 1 s intervals) to capture transient spikes. | Detect micro‑latency spikes that may be missed at longer intervals. |
| **Baseline Modeling** | Build a statistical baseline (mean, std dev) for latency and error rates. | Enables anomaly detection and early warning of deviations. |
| **Capacity Planning** | Simulate traffic growth (e.g., 10×) to identify when latency or packet loss may begin to rise. | Proactive scaling of bandwidth or resources. |
| **Application Profiling** | Profile the backend to identify any CPU‑bound or I/O‑bound operations. | Reduces the Postman response time gap. |
| **Caching** | Implement or tune HTTP caching (ETag, Cache‑Control) for idempotent endpoints. | Lowers server load and reduces round‑trip latency. |
| **Connection Management** | Use HTTP/2 or HTTP/3 where supported to multiplex requests over a single connection. | Reduces connection establishment overhead and improves throughput. |
| **Redundancy & Failover** | Verify that failover mechanisms (e.g., active‑passive clusters) are tested and that health checks are in place. | Ensures continued health if a component fails. |
| **Security** | Ensure TLS termination is off‑loaded to dedicated hardware or use TLS 1.3. | Reduces CPU overhead and latency for encrypted traffic. |
| **Network QoS** | Apply QoS policies to prioritize critical traffic (e.g., real‑time services). | Guarantees latency for high‑priority flows even under load. |
